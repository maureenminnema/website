In this study, we investigate how lucid dream can be induced effectively in the at-home sleeping environment. The study uses wearable two-channel EEG, an automated sleep-scoring model to detect sleep stages in real time, and custom software to deliver cues according to the participants' sleep stage. The protocol is fully automated and does not require experimenter supervision at night, but it's success highly depends on the quality of the instructions participants receive to be able to perform the study at home. In my role as research assistant, I coordinate all logistics and participant instructions, monitoring and support, i.e. scheduling; communication; explaining experimental procedures, as well as monitoring data quality.

The [pre-registration](https://osf.io/download/69b2bb39449d6d94a7dc7899/) is available. This study has been presented during the poster session at ESRS Sleep Europe Conference 2026 ([read the abstract, page 73)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/epdf/10.1111/jsr.70435#page=73)).
